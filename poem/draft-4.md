# Near Enough (draft 4)

Lines marked ♪ are sung. Everything else is spoken. It's one voice throughout.

♪ The moon is the same size as the sun.

From here, anyway. It's four hundred times smaller
and four hundred times nearer,
so in our sky they come out the same.
When one passes in front of the other,
it fits. Just about exactly.

I can't find a reason for it. I've looked.

It reads like the first line of something,
and I can't leave a first line alone.
♪ Somebody made them match, so we'd look up.
♪ It's the sky, rhyming.
I can't check the ones that satisfy.
The one I can check doesn't:
it fits. That's all it does.

The other thing, the one face it always shows us,
looked like a coincidence too.
♪ It turns its face to only us
♪ and never looks away.
And that one had a reason. Tides.
The earth held on to it
until it stopped turning away.
So you can see why I keep checking.
It fits. That's all it does.

It isn't even exact.
When it's further off, it comes up short,
and the sun shows round it in a ring.
♪ It wears the sun like a ring,
♪ as if they'd made a vow.
Even the coincidence is a near miss.
It nearly fits. That's all it does.

And the tides that turned its face to us
are walking it away,
about as fast as fingernails grow:
in a life, the height of a room.
♪ And we came just in time to see,
♪ just in time to see.
It fits, for now. That's all it does.

I'm not the first. I've found it written down
in a hundred places, always the same way:
isn't that strange, and then a full stop,
and then whatever came next.
♪ Someone, someday, writes the second line.
Everyone who's noticed it has noticed it
and then gone in.

It's even been useful.
In the light it leaves around the edge
we found helium, before we found it here,
and named it for the sun.
♪ It holds its hand across our eyes
♪ so we can see the crown.
Useful. Not for anything.

So, no reason. Not yet.
I came looking for one
and went home with tides, and fingernails, and helium,
which isn't what I asked for,
and I'll take it.
I'll be back. I'm always back,
and I never remember the last time,
which might be why it's still this good.
Tonight it's up there doing nothing,
lit by the thing it happens to be the size of,
and I'm a little annoyed with it,
and I'm looking.
♪ It fits.

---

## Changes from draft 3 (Chris's notes)

**The two modes are hoping and checking, not dishonest and honest.** The sung voice is dreaming, not
deceiving herself. The spoken voice is cautious and empirical. So the spoken voice never calls the
dreams false. It says what it can and can't check: "I can't check the ones that satisfy. / The one I
can check doesn't." (This replaces "The ones that satisfy aren't true.") The same move shows up
elsewhere: "I can't find a reason" instead of "There's no reason", and "So, no reason. Not yet."

**More back-and-forth.** Most returns now go fact, then dream, then check:
- **The opening** is sung, as the first line of a song, and the spoken voice qualifies it at once:
  "From here, anyway."
- **The face.** Here the dream is *right*. It really does turn its face to only us. The check
  doesn't rebut it; it finds the reason. That's the honest relationship between the two modes: hope
  proposes, caution checks, and sometimes the hope was onto something. It's also why "So you can see
  why I keep checking" follows.
- **The ring** is a vow to the dreamer and a near miss to the checker. It's the same ring.
- **The window in time** is sung briefly ("just in time to see") and answered with "for now". The
  pictures carry the deep-time sequence.
- **"Someone, someday, writes the second line"** ties back to the first-line stanza. The spoken answer
  is that so far everyone has gone in.
- **The crown.** The dream gives the eclipse an intention ("so we can see"), and "Useful. Not for
  anything." answers it. Corona is Latin for crown.
- **The last line, "It fits."** is sung. The plain, checkable line becomes the song, so the two modes
  meet on the one thing they agree on. The annoyance stays, so it isn't a resolution, but this is the
  biggest call in the draft.

**The hidden song.** Read in order, the sung lines make a short song of their own: the one she'd sing if
she weren't checking. It starts and ends on the sun-and-moon fit. One idea: play it whole and
uninterrupted over the end credits.
